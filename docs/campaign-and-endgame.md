# Campaign and Endgame — The Meridian Project

## Overview

The main campaign culminates in the **Meridian Core** and the **Meridian Gate**. The Core is a high-prestige crafted artifact, but the Gate is the actual project: a multi-subsystem structure that must be assembled, supplied, and commissioned.

The player should understand the Gate's purpose long before they can build it. Early exploration introduces fragments, schematics, or records that foreshadow later industrial requirements.

## Campaign acts

### Act I — Survey the Frontier
**Purpose:** Establish survival foundations and introduce the mystery.

Possible objectives:
- Establish a safe base and basic resource supply.
- Find the dormant Gate site or recover its first survey record.
- Identify deposits and materials needed for industrial development.
- Complete a first basic power and processing setup.

Avoid making rare exploration loot a hard progression wall before the player has the tools to explore.

### Act II — Found the Works
**Purpose:** Introduce the two core mods as complementary technologies.

Possible objectives:
- Establish coke/charcoal and treated-material production.
- Produce the first reliable steel supply.
- Build IE power generation and distribution.
- Establish basic Mekanism processing.
- Automate one repeatable material chain.

### Act III — Industrial Expansion
**Purpose:** Move from isolated machines to dedicated production facilities.

Possible objectives:
- Expand ore processing and material refinement.
- Establish chemical production and safe gas/fluid handling.
- Manufacture advanced alloys and engineered components.
- Introduce conveyors, pipes, storage, and transport choices.
- Complete at least one meaningful cross-mod production chain.

### Act IV — The Grid
**Purpose:** Make the player build an industrial network rather than a single room of machines.

The four working districts are:
1. **Resource Basin** — extraction, sorting, primary processing, and bulk storage.
2. **Chemical Works** — chemical inputs, gas processing, fluid handling, and specialty materials.
3. **Power Station** — generation, conversion, storage, and distribution.
4. **Central Foundry** — steel, alloys, precision assemblies, and final manufacturing.

These are thematic regions, not mandatory coordinates or fixed base layouts. A player may combine districts if their build supports the required production.

### Act V — The Meridian Project
**Purpose:** Manufacture the five Core component families and construct the Gate subsystems.

## Meridian Core: component design

| Component | Industrial challenge | Intended role |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial Heart | Heavy fabrication, steel/alloy supply, large assemblies | IE-led structural and mechanical engineering |
| Chemical Matrix | Advanced gases, fluids, synthesis, and material transformation | Mekanism-led chemical engineering |
| Power Singularity | Large-scale generation, storage, conversion, and stable supply | Shared power infrastructure |
| Control Lattice | Precision components, automation, and logistics | Shared manufacturing and control |
| Astral Keystone | Exploration, recovered artifacts, and frontier research | Exploration-driven unlock |

Each component should have its own quest chapter, intermediate subassemblies, and a clear explanation of why its production chain exists. Ingredient and quantity decisions remain open until the actual mod versions and recipes are tested.

### Act VI — Gate construction and commissioning

The Gate should have multiple distinct subsystems, for example:
- **Frame and Stabilizers:** structural materials and heavy fabrication.
- **Power Coupling:** high-capacity energy interface and storage.
- **Chemical/Material Conduits:** specialized fluids, gases, and manufactured components.
- **Control Assembly:** Core, precision components, and activation controls.
- **Destination Lock:** recovered information or a crafted keystone that defines the first destination.

The quest system can track submitted components and completed recipes. If the final structure requires a physical build, use a documented player-certified checklist unless a tested structure detector or custom multiblock implementation is available.

Commissioning should be a memorable sequence: complete final deliveries, activate the control block or custom interaction, play a sound/visual event, and unlock the Gate destination. The exact mechanic depends on the selected implementation route.

### Act VII — Beyond the Gate

The first destination should be more than a resource quarry. It should provide a new reason to explore and build:
- Recoverable remnants of the lost civilization.
- New environmental or logistical challenges.
- A limited set of unique materials with clear uses.
- A place to establish an outpost.
- Optional routes to improve or expand the Gate.

## Post-game projects

These are aspirational and optional:
- Build a self-sufficient frontier outpost.
- Establish automated transport between the original base and the frontier.
- Manufacture additional Meridian-grade components.
- Build a second-generation power and control system.
- Complete large-scale industrial contracts with escalating but sustainable production goals.

Do not make post-game bulk quotas mandatory for the main ending.

## Fair-grind rules

- Bulk materials must be renewable or automatable before they become large recurring requirements.
- Unique exploration drops should be guaranteed through a discoverable source, a bounded loot table, or a deterministic structure reward—not an opaque low-probability grind.
- Avoid hard gates that require a machine tier that itself requires the gated item.
- A major component should combine multiple production chains, not merely multiply one ingot.
- Provide alternatives when a requirement would otherwise force a single mod or machine with no meaningful choice.
- Make quest text state the expected source and purpose of unusual ingredients.
