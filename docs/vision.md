# MERIDIAN — Vision and Design Principles

## The fantasy

The player inherits a remote industrial frontier and discovers a dormant gateway: a monumental machine whose original civilization, power system, and destination are unknown. The Gate is not repaired by finding one magic key. It requires an entire industrial ecosystem.

The player gradually establishes extraction, refining, chemical works, heavy fabrication, power generation, storage, and logistics. Those facilities ultimately manufacture the Meridian Core and the parts needed to reconstruct the Gate.

The tone should be **industrial wonder**: grounded engineering that grows into something extraordinary.

## What MERIDIAN is

- A technology-focused campaign with a clear long-term objective.
- A pack where IE's visible infrastructure and multiblocks complement Mekanism's advanced processing and utilities.
- A factory-building experience where automation makes ambitious goals fair.
- A curated survival world with exploration that feeds into, but does not replace, industrial progression.
- A project with bespoke identity: custom items, names, textures, quest presentation, and eventually custom mechanics where justified.

## What MERIDIAN is not

- A kitchen-sink pack with unrelated mods added without a role.
- A race to the highest tier of one mod.
- A pile of inflated crafting costs.
- A quest book that pretends it can inspect machines or measure production when it cannot.
- A dimension pack where the destination is the only interesting content.
- A pack whose performance assumptions exceed the target hardware without a clear reason.

## Design rules

### 1. Every major cost has a purpose
A component should be expensive because it combines meaningful production systems, not because a recipe multiplies an ordinary item by 64. Explain the role of each expensive material in the quest text.

### 2. Production beats manual repetition
A player should be able to automate every recurring bulk requirement. Rare exploration items may be finite, but should unlock systems or milestones rather than become an endless random-drop tax.

### 3. Both pillars matter
IE should contribute more than decorative early-game machines. Mekanism should contribute more than a late-game ore multiplier. Cross-mod chains must be designed and tested deliberately.

### 4. Progress is visible
Use named contracts, subsystem milestones, diagrams, and clear quest dependencies. Each Core component should feel like a project completed, not a row of ingredients.

### 5. Respect player choice
Where possible, permit multiple valid power, logistics, or production designs. Avoid requiring one highly specific base layout unless a real custom mechanic supports it.

### 6. Separate campaign completion from endless play
The Meridian Gate activation is the main ending. Frontier expansion and second-generation engineering are optional post-game goals.

## Player experience targets

- Early game: resourcefulness, first power, first steel, first automated processing.
- Mid game: factories become specialized; chemical and material chains interlock.
- Late game: the player designs a reliable industrial grid and manufactures high-spec components.
- Finale: several completed industrial systems converge into one coherent megaproject.
- Post-game: the player chooses how far to scale the frontier.

## Scope guardrails

The first playable release should focus on a stable Overworld campaign and a complete industrial progression. A custom destination dimension is desirable but should be treated as a separate, gated milestone. If a suitable existing dimension mod proves more reliable and thematically appropriate, it may be used as an interim or final destination.
